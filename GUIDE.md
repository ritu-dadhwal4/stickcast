# Make money with faceless stick-figure videos

A practical guide by **Ritu Dadhwal**, for people who want to build a YouTube channel (and Shorts, Reels and TikTok) with stick-figure videos made in **stickcast**, without ever showing their face.

This guide is honest: it shows what works, how the money actually arrives, and the rules that get channels demonetized. It doesn't promise income, because nobody can.

---

## 1. Why stick-figure videos work

- **They are cheap to make.** No camera, studio, actors or editing suite. A script and stickcast are enough.
- **They are clear.** A stick figure pulling a lever or watching a chart grow explains an idea faster than a talking head. Viewers stay longer, and watch time is what YouTube rewards.
- **They travel.** Stick figures have no language, age or country. Re-voice the same video in Hindi or Spanish and it works again.
- **They fit every format.** The same story becomes a 6-minute YouTube video, a 45-second Short and an Instagram Reel.
- **The niche is proven.** Stick-figure explainers, relatable comedy shorts, versus fights and "escape" compilations regularly reach millions of views. Videos in this style pass 5, 8 and even 20+ million views.

## 2. Pick your niche

Choose one lane and stay in it for at least 30 videos. Your cast and colour theme become your brand.

| Niche | Example videos | Best format | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal finance | "Why saving early beats earning more", "5 money mistakes in your 20s" | 16:9 explainers + Shorts | Advertisers pay more for money topics |
| Science and how things work | "What happens when you crack your knuckles", "How a plane stays up" | 16:9 explainers | Evergreen: earns for years |
| School and student life | "POV: the teacher says pick a partner", "Lunch break memories" | 9:16 Shorts | Very shareable, huge audience |
| Relationships and dating jokes | "Dating in 2026 be like", "When she says 'I'm fine'" | 9:16 Shorts | Comments and shares drive reach |
| Psychology and self-improvement | "Why you procrastinate", "The 2-minute rule" | 16:9 + Shorts | Strong watch time |
| History in 60 seconds | "Why the Titanic sank", "The shortest war ever" | Shorts + long compilations | Curiosity hooks |
| Tech and careers | "How the internet works", "What a product manager does" | 16:9 explainers | Good sponsors |
| Action and versus | "Strength vs speed", "Red vs blue: the rematch" | Shorts, no narration | Rewatchable, global |

**Be careful with health, medical and financial *advice*.** Explain ideas and cite real sources; don't tell people what to buy or take. YouTube is strict here, especially with automated voices.

## 3. The four formats that win

These are the patterns behind the big stick-figure channels. Make your own versions with your own characters.

1. **The explainer.** A hook question, then one idea per scene with a prop that shows it (a board, a chart, coins, a calendar), ending on a payoff. *Try:* `/stickcast how credit card interest traps you, 4 minutes`
2. **The relatable POV short.** "POV:" plus a situation everyone knows, three beats and a twist. *Try:* `/stickcast 30 second Short: POV you said "you too" when the waiter said enjoy your meal, doodle style`
3. **The versus fight.** Two colours, a clear rule (strength vs speed, brains vs muscle), quick choreography and a surprise winner. No voice needed. *Try:* `/stickcast red vs blue, strength vs speed, 20 seconds, no narration, with a POW`
4. **The challenge or escape.** Four coloured characters, four coloured doors or pipes, and each one solves it differently. Great as a long compilation. *Try:* `/stickcast 4 colour friends must escape a room using only their colour, 60 seconds`

## 4. Make your first video with stickcast

1. Install the skill (see the README) and open Claude Code.
2. Type `/stickcast` and your topic. Say whether you want a long video (16:9) or a Short (9:16).
3. **Approve the script.** Check the hook: would *you* keep watching after line one?
4. **Pick a colour theme** on the theme page. Pick one you'll keep for the whole channel.
5. **Storyboard:** say yes for your first few videos, then skip it once you trust the flow.
6. **Pick a voice.** Your own recorded voice builds the most trust. A free Mac voice is fine to start with.
7. **Pick a caption style.** "Pop" works best for Shorts and "subtitle" for long videos.
8. Watch the preview, ask for any changes, and get the MP4.
9. **Approve the thumbnail.** stickcast also gives you a title, description and tags to paste.

Your first video will take the longest. By video ten you'll know which steps you can say "approved" to straight away.

## 5. Set up your channel

- **Name:** short and about the promise, not about you: *Money in Minutes*, *Stick Science*, *Class Clowns*.
- **Brand:** one theme, one cast and the same accessories in every video. Viewers should recognise your channel from a thumbnail.
- **Banner and profile picture:** use a stickcast thumbnail of your main character.
- **About section:** one line on what you post and how often.
- **Playlists:** group videos by topic, which keeps people watching.

## 6. Titles, thumbnails and hooks

**Titles** (under 60 characters) that work in this niche:
- *The ___ trick nobody tells you*
- *Why ___ is a scam*
- *What happens if you ___ every day*
- *POV: ___*
- *___ vs ___*

**Thumbnails:** 3 to 6 big words, one word in the highlight colour, and one character with a strong expression (shocked, laughing, crying). Leave space between text and figure. stickcast's thumbnail step follows these rules.

**Hooks:** the first 2 seconds decide everything. Open with a question, a surprising number or the punchline setup, never with "Hi guys, welcome back".

## 7. A posting rhythm that grows

- **Shorts:** 1 a day if you can, at least 4 a week. They bring new subscribers.
- **Long videos:** 1 a week. They bring watch hours and most of the ad money.
- **Repurpose everything:** cut each long video into 2-3 vertical Shorts (stickcast can restage a scene in 9:16), then post them to Instagram Reels, TikTok and Facebook too.
- **Read your numbers weekly in YouTube Studio:** click-through rate (is the thumbnail working?), average view duration (is the script working?), and where viewers drop off (which scene lost them?). Change one thing at a time.

## 8. How the money arrives

### YouTube Partner Program (ads)

As of late 2026, YouTube has two levels. Always check the current numbers in **YouTube Studio → Earn**, because they change.

| Level | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Fan funding | 500 subscribers and 3,000 watch hours in 12 months (or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days), plus 3 public uploads in 90 days | Memberships, Super Thanks, Shopping |
| Ad revenue | 1,000 subscribers and **4,000 watch hours in 12 months**, or **10 million Shorts views in 90 days** | A share of ad money on long videos and Shorts |

Shorts views don't count toward the 4,000 watch hours. That's why the winning mix is **Shorts for growth plus long videos for watch hours**. YouTube has announced higher ad-revenue thresholds for new applicants from February 2027, while channels already in the program keep their status. Getting in early helps.

How much a view pays depends heavily on the niche and the viewer's country. Finance, tech and education pay much more per view than comedy, and long videos pay far more per view than Shorts.

### Beyond ads (often bigger)

- **Sponsorships:** once you have a steady audience, apps and services in your niche pay for a 30-60 second mention. A stick character "using" their product fits naturally.
- **Affiliate links:** link the books, tools or apps you mention in the description.
- **Selling explainer videos:** companies, schools, coaches and startups need short animated explainers. Your channel is your portfolio, and stickcast makes the videos. Charge per finished minute.
- **Digital products:** turn your best explainers into a course, an e-book or printable worksheets.
- **Other platforms:** TikTok, Instagram and Facebook each have their own creator programs. Post the same vertical videos there.

## 9. Stay monetizable (read this twice)

YouTube does not pay for **"inauthentic content"**: mass-produced, repetitive or template videos with little original value. AI tools are allowed; low-effort copies are not. To stay safe:

- **Write original scripts** with a real point of view, your own jokes and checked facts. Don't paste the same template 100 times with a new word swapped in.
- **Vary your videos:** different scenes, layouts and stories. stickcast gives you many backgrounds, props and poses, so use them.
- **Add a human touch:** your own voice, your own opinions, replies to comments in a follow-up video.
- **Never re-voice or re-upload someone else's animation.** Channels that dub famous animators' videos live on borrowed content and can lose everything to one claim. Make your own.
- **Don't use famous characters, game worlds or brand mascots,** even redrawn as stick figures.
- **Use licensed music only:** the YouTube Audio Library or music you've paid for.
- **Fact-check money and health claims,** and say clearly that it's education, not advice.
- **Synthetic content labels** are for realistic AI people, places and events. Stick-figure animation isn't realistic, but if you ever add realistic AI footage, tick the label in YouTube Studio.

## 10. Your 30-day launch plan

| Days | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Pick your niche, channel name, theme and cast. Make the banner and profile picture with stickcast. |
| 3-5 | Write 10 Short scripts and 2 long-video scripts. Study 5 top videos in your niche and write down their hooks. |
| 6-10 | Make and post 1 Short a day. Make long video #1. |
| 11-15 | Post long video #1 and keep up 1 Short a day. Reply to every comment. |
| 16-20 | Check retention: which scenes lose viewers? Make long video #2 with shorter scenes there. |
| 21-25 | Cut 3 Shorts from your long videos, and start posting on Reels and TikTok too. |
| 26-30 | Double down on the 3 best-performing ideas. Plan next month around them. |

Most channels need months, not weeks. The people who make money are the ones who are still posting at video 100.
